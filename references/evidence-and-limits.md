## Primary sources and application

- [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) is the web accessibility baseline reference. This practitioner Guide supplements it and does not certify conformance.
- [Making Content Usable for People with Cognitive and Learning Disabilities](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/) informs orientation, understandable content and task support. Guide rules are not automatically formal WCAG criteria.
- [WAI-Adapt overview](https://www.w3.org/WAI/adapt/) describes adaptation to individual needs and distinguishes documents at different maturity levels. It informs the direction; these examples do not implement WAI-Adapt semantics or claim interoperability with its modules.
- [AASPIRE](https://aaspire.org/) describes community-based participatory research with autistic people. It supports involving intended users in future evaluation; it is not evidence that this project's CSS settings suit AuDHD users.
- [Content on hover or focus](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/content-on-hover-or-focus.html), [focus visible](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-visible.html), and [use of color](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html) support the corresponding accessibility checks. A tooltip library or token table alone does not establish the result.
- Library documentation and provisional integration mappings are maintained in the [adapter reference](framework-adapters.md).

The two references can share a design mistake despite independent state engines. Automated assertions, visual inspection, assistive-technology testing, model evaluation and participant research are different evidence types. Report each separately. Actual browser zoom, screen-reader operation, representative participant evaluation and backend persistence remain outside the recorded automated evidence.
